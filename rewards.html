Points are a record, not a currency

Run Points are how the app remembers what you did. Every one of them is tied to a specific action — a run you completed, an order you confirmed, a store you helped build out. You can open any run and see the exact lines that produced its points.

How points become $RUN

A season closes on a snapshot. After that moment nothing recalculates — your total is frozen, signed, and paid. Here is the whole conversion, in order.

01
Your points are adjusted for boosts. Any multipliers you qualified for at snapshot time are applied to what you earned.
02
Rank tilts the result. The season leaderboard is bracketed — the top of the board converts at a higher rate than the middle, and the middle higher than the rest. Running more moves you up.
03
Points convert at a published rate. A set number of points equals one $RUN. This is the part that matters: you receive what you earned — not a slice of a pot that gets handed out regardless.
04
A season pool caps the total. Each season has a maximum. If a season is busy enough that earnings would exceed it, everyone is scaled back by the same proportion — never first-come-first-served.
05
What isn't earned is never minted. If a season's activity only earns a fraction of the pool, only that fraction is created. The rest doesn't get distributed to anyone — it simply never comes into existence.

That last point is the design decision we care most about. A rewards pool that always empties itself teaches people that showing up doesn't matter. This one only pays for work actually done.

Relics — why last season's NFT still matters

A season NFT that stops doing anything the moment its season ends is a NFT that quietly becomes worthless. That's not the deal. When a Dash Season collection's season closes, it becomes a Relic — and Relics keep working.

01
Set bonus, permanently. Holding three or more from a single collection earns a flat points bonus at every future snapshot — not just during that collection's own season.
02
Collector bonus across seasons. Holding Relics from multiple distinct past seasons earns an additional bonus. Only elapsed seasons count, so the set you can complete grows over time — and the only way to get an old season's Relic is from someone who already holds one.
03
Provenance that can't be reissued. Sequential token IDs and a fixed per-season supply mean an early season is permanently early. Nothing gets minted back into a closed season.
04
You can always sell. Relics are ERC-721s on Ronin, tradeable on Mavis Market. Secondary sales carry a 5% royalty — half of which goes back to whoever originally minted that token, so minting early keeps paying.

We're publishing these mechanics before the first Season NFT is ever minted, deliberately. Nobody should buy one and find out the rules afterwards.

Launch boosts — these have an end date

Holding Axies, or holding $RON, earns a launch boost today. Both are deliberately temporary: they exist to welcome the Ronin community, and they retire when Dash Genesis arrives. We would rather tell you that now than surprise you later. The durable boosts are Dash Genesis and holding $RUN.

$RUN supply, and how it enters the world

The supply is fixed and the release schedule is slow on purpose. Rewards should still mean something in season twenty.

Total supply1,000,000,000 $RUN — fixed Maximum released per season1,000,000 $RUN Share of supply per season0.1%
01
Fixed supply, no inflation switch. One billion $RUN, total. There is no mechanism to mint beyond it.
02
Earned into circulation, not sold into it. The season pool is the release valve, and it only opens for points people actually earned. A quiet season releases very little — the unearned remainder is never created.
03
The pool is re-declared each season. One million is the current ceiling per season, not a guarantee, and it is set openly before a season opens — never adjusted after a snapshot.
04
Revenue flows back. A share of NFT margin and service-fee revenue is used for treasury operations including market purchases of $RUN. This is discretionary treasury policy, not a commitment, a floor, or a promise about price.

At 0.1% per season, releasing the full supply through rewards alone would take on the order of a thousand seasons — and only if every season were maxed out, which the earn-rate design makes unlikely by construction.

The treasury, and who should steer it

RetailRun takes a flat 10% service fee. As the network grows, part of what the ecosystem generates is intended to accumulate in a treasury rather than being fully extracted.

—
Where it comes from. Undistributed season supply, a share of service-fee revenue, and proceeds from NFT sales and secondary royalties.
—
What it's for. Funding future season pools, supporting liquidity so earned $RUN is actually usable, and paying for growth of the network itself.
—
Where it's going. Our intent is that people who build the network get a real say in how that treasury is used — the runners doing the work, not just the founders.

Straight talk about governance

Community governance is our stated direction, not a shipped feature and not a promise. Nothing here grants a right to profits, revenue, or control, and the mechanics are subject to change as we work through the legal and regulatory picture. When any part of it becomes real, it will be described here in plain language before it launches — the same way we told you the launch boosts expire.

Why we build on Ronin

We needed a chain where a person receiving a small reward isn't punished for it. Most aren't. Ronin is.

Fees that don't eat the reward

Getting paid a few dollars of rewards is pointless if the transfer costs more than the reward. Ronin's fees are small enough that a season payout arrives intact.

Built for real users, not traders

Ronin was built for Axie Infinity — millions of ordinary people, many on phones, many who had never touched crypto. That's our user too.

A wallet people already have

Ronin Wallet is mainstream in this community. We'd rather meet people where they already are than make them learn a new tool to collect a reward.

A community that gets it

Ronin users already understand earning inside an app and owning the result. We didn't have to invent that expectation — and we boost Axie holders as thanks.

Your rewards settle in a wallet you control. Once a season pays out, the tokens are yours — we can't claw them back, freeze them, or change the amount after the snapshot.
